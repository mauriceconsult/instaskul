# Instaskul's Journey: From a Downtown Tutor's Dream to Africa's Teaching Revolution

**February 2026** - *A story 17 years in the making*

---

## The Spark: Downtown Nairobi, 2009

Picture this: Kenya's downtown colleges, 2009. Facebook was taking over. Feature phones were giving way to touchscreens. And I, a tutor working seven days a week, watched as my profession remained stuck in the past.

While lawyers opened consultancies and doctors built private practices, **we tutors had no path forward.** No time for side projects. No professional mobility. Just seven-day workweeks teaching British curricula we hadn't designed.

But something was brewing beneath the surface.

---

## The Ignored Request

In a staff meeting that year, I made a request that would change my life—even though nobody acknowledged it at the time.

*"Can we set up an online learning department?"*

Silence.

The thing is, **I didn't even know how to code.** I had no programming background, no budget, no team. Just a growing frustration with the status quo and a vague vision of something better.

When the meeting ended with my request unacknowledged, I shrunk back into routine. Rent was due. Bills needed paying. The idea retreated into my subconscious.

But ideas like this don't die—they wait.

---

## The Waiting Years: 2010-2019

**2010:** Lost my downtown job. Started graduate school. Got my first personal computer.

**2011:** Joined a career consulting firm with a former colleague.

**2013:** After speaking at a seminar in Kampala, I stayed. Opened a small computer services shop. Connected with a local university.

**2015-2018:** Taught at campuses in Kampala and Juba. The computer shop closed, but the teaching continued.

Through it all, that 2009 idea kept whispering: *There has to be a better way.*

---

## COVID Changed Everything

When COVID-19 hit in 2020, I found myself back at my computer with time to think.

I Googled "digital teaching opportunities East Africa."

**The results? Nothing.**

Not a single platform built *for* African teachers, *by* African teachers.

That's when it clicked: **If we're going to wait for someone else to build it, we'll be waiting forever.**

In 2021, I started drafting a learning management system.

---

## The Brutal Truth: 2022-2023

In 2022, I built a low-code prototype and shared it with people.

**Their response? Crickets.**

It was so primitive and unstable that nobody who saw it acknowledged it. Sound familiar? Just like that 2009 staff meeting.

But this time, I didn't shrink back.

In 2023, **I learned to code.** Python. Java. Whatever it took.

I built the first real draft of what would become Instaskul. Through 2024, I iterated, debugged, refined. By December 2024, it was 80% complete.

---

## The AI Plot Twist

Here's something I never saw coming: **AI would revolutionize how I built Instaskul.**

In early 2025, VS Code's inline suggestions started getting eerily accurate. It felt like finally having the team member I'd been searching for.

By mid-2025, AI-assisted debugging became my norm. **The product we have today—sleek, powerful, functional—would have been impossible without AI.**

But here's the key: **AI didn't build Instaskul. I did.**

AI was the tool. The vision, the persistence, the 17 years of lived experience as an African educator—that's what built Instaskul.
